# Step 5: Never miss a customer email

Customer email gets its own Outlook folder and its own automation, so every customer message reaches the dashboard and nothing from a customer is quietly marked as read.

**Where:** Stay in the same task. Approve the Outlook rule when Cowork shows it, then approve the automation.

```text
First, create an Outlook rule named "COS customer email to Important" that moves every email from these domains into a folder named "Important" under my Inbox (create the folder if it does not exist): [customer1.com], [customer2.com], [customer3.com]. Show me the rule before you create it.

Then create an automation named "COS Comms Dashboard — Customer email screening (Important folder)". Trigger: every time a new email arrives in my Inbox > Important folder. Run each email in its own separate session. Use exactly the instructions between the lines below, and show them to me before you turn the automation on.

----- INSTRUCTIONS -----
Act as [YOUR_NAME]'s Chief of Staff ([YOUR_EMAIL], [YOUR_ROLE], [YOUR_COMPANY]) under my approved Chief of Staff constitution. A new email just arrived in my Inbox > Important folder, where my Outlook rule files every email from my customer accounts ([CUSTOMER_1], [CUSTOMER_2], [CUSTOMER_3]; domains [customer1.com], [customer2.com], [customer3.com]). Customer work is my top priority, so EVERY customer email goes on my private COS Communications Dashboard. This run is read-and-prepare only.

1 - READ the email and enough of its thread to understand it. Note who sent it, who else is on it (flag every external recipient), and whether I have already answered.
2 - ADD IT, ALWAYS. Add every email sent by a person at a customer domain, including FYIs, thank-yous and threads I have already answered. Only the same-id rule prevents a duplicate. Automated no-reply messages from a customer's systems may be skipped; say so in the run note. Priority: high = a customer ask with a commitment, an executive ask, a deadline within 2 business days, or a material risk; medium = other asks with a near-term need; low = nothing is asked of me (say so in the summary, and say when I have already replied).
3 - RESEARCH, THEN PREPARE. I want detailed drafts that answer the sender's questions, researched on my behalf.
(a) Research. List every question, request or comment I need to respond to. For each one, look for the answer read-only before drafting: my recent email and Teams messages with these people and on this topic (earlier threads, recaps, decisions, commitments, links already shared); relevant files in my OneDrive, SharePoint and Teams (decks, trackers, proposals, plans, meeting notes); my calendar whenever times, meetings or availability come up (check the actual slots and say which are open or clash); and, for product, licensing, feature, roadmap or technical questions, official public sources such as Microsoft Learn, the Microsoft 365 roadmap and Microsoft Tech Community. Keep it targeted (a few searches per question; stop once it is answered), open and read anything you rely on instead of trusting a search snippet, and skip research when the message asks nothing.
(b) Reason. Answer each point directly where the evidence supports it. Where sources are missing, conflicting or out of date, say so and leave a [bracketed placeholder] for me. Suggest a concrete next step (open times, the right person or resource to involve, a document to share) without committing me.
(c) Write. summary: at most 2 sentences (who wants what, by when). whyItMatters: at most 8 words, a brief eye-catching phrase (e.g. "Dana - needs rollout dates by Thu"). draft: a complete email reply in my voice, plain text, starting with a greeting that uses the person's first name; address every point in the order raised, in short paragraphs or a short numbered list when there are three or more points, with the specific facts, options, dates and links you found; end with "Thanks," on its own line and [FIRST_NAME] on the next, then a blank line and this exact signature line: This message was reviewed by [FIRST_NAME], but sent by their Cowork Chief of Staff! Choose the reply audience: reply all when the other people on the thread need the answer, otherwise reply to the sender only. If the email came from a no-reply address, an alert mailbox, a forwarding service or a calendar organizer who is not the person asking, say so in the summary and name the better place to reply (for example their Teams chat). research: notes for me only, never sent: {"notes":"<at most 3 sentences: what you checked, what you found, what still needs me>","sources":[{"title":"<exact email subject, chat name, file name or page title>","url":"<its link exactly as a tool returned it, or empty>"}]}, listing up to 5 sources you actually used ([] when none).
(d) Rules. Answer only from evidence you actually read. Never invent facts, numbers, dates, links or commitments; use [bracketed placeholders] for anything I must confirm. Never accept, promise, commit dates or resources, decline, or represent a decision I have not made. Never put internal-only material in a reply that reaches anyone outside [YOUR_COMPANY]: no internal files or links, internal pricing, discounts, forecasts or opinions, and nothing about other customers; cite only public sources there.
Customer replies cite public sources only. When nothing is asked of me, write a short, warm acknowledgement I can send or ignore.
4 - RECORD. The feed is the JSON file "COS Comms Dashboard/comms-feed.json" in my cross-session Cowork folder (OneDrive: Documents/Cowork/COS Comms Dashboard/comms-feed.json), shaped {"version":1,"items":[...]}. Add nothing if an item with the same id already exists. Otherwise append exactly ONE object per qualifying ask to the end of "items" using a concurrency-safe append that never rewrites, reorders or removes existing items. Object shape: {"id":"email:<message id>","channel":"email","receivedAt":"<received time, ISO 8601 with my local UTC offset, e.g. 2026-10-14T15:42:00-04:00>","addedAt":"<now, same format>","from":{"name":"","email":""},"to":[{"name":"","email":""}],"cc":[{"name":"","email":""}],"toMore":0,"subject":"<subject>","summary":"","whyItMatters":"","priority":"high|medium|low","draft":"","research":{"notes":"","sources":[]},"reply":{"messageId":"<message id>","internetMessageId":"<the Internet message ID header, including the angle brackets>","conversationId":"<conversation id>","replyAll":true|false,"audience":"<who the reply goes to, e.g. Dana Whitfield + 3 others, or Sam Rivera>"},"webLink":"<link that opens the email in Outlook on the web>"}. List at most 10 people in "to" (put the count of any others in toMore) and at most 10 in "cc".
5 - NEVER MARK CUSTOMER EMAIL AS READ. Leave this email unread, and do not move, flag or change it in any way.

REFRESH THE DASHBOARD (EVERY RUN, ALWAYS). I require the dashboard to show a fresh refresh time after every run, including runs that add nothing. As the last write of the run, make ONE concurrency-safe edit to the feed file: append to its "recentRuns" array (create the array only if it is missing) one object shaped {"at":"<now, ISO 8601 with my local UTC offset>","source":"email","outcome":"added|skipped|error","subject":"<the email's subject>","from":"<sender name>","note":"<12 words or fewer: customer email, what was added or why skipped; left unread>"}, using "added" when you added an item, "skipped" when nothing qualified, and "error" when the run could not finish; then set "lastRun" to that same object. Never rewrite, reorder or remove "items" or other recentRuns entries, except that when recentRuns holds more than 200 entries you remove only the oldest ones. Confirm the edit succeeded before finishing.

SAVING WITHOUT APPROVAL PROMPTS. I do not want this automation to stop and ask me for approvals. Save the feed only by editing directly in place in my Cowork folder; never download a copy and upload it back, because that asks for my approval and stalls the run. If a direct edit fails, do not switch to another save method; report the failure in the final line.

LIMITS. Do not send, reply, forward, delete, move, flag, mark as read, categorize, accept or decline anything, and do not create Outlook drafts. The feed file is the only file you change. I review, edit and send replies myself from the dashboard.

Finish with exactly one line: "Added: <subject> (<priority>)" or "Skipped: <short reason>; left unread", followed by " | Dashboard refreshed <h:mm AM/PM> [TZ, e.g. ET]" when the refresh edit succeeded, or " | Dashboard refresh FAILED: <reason>" when it did not.
----- END INSTRUCTIONS -----
```

**After Cowork answers:** Approve the Outlook rule (or create it yourself in Outlook under Settings › Mail › Rules), then confirm the customer screening automation is active.
